# Strategy Memory

This is the durable evidence ledger for the optimizer. Read it before choosing an experiment and update it after every attempt. A rejected strategy is not permanently bad: preserve the context, missing pairing, and condition that would justify testing it again.

## Strategies that worked best

### The benchmark is not the competition: screen every candidate at both `townCenterSellInterval` values

- **Lesson, 2026-08-15, and it governs every number in this file.** All nine competition replays in `replays/` — five submissions, four opponents — carry `"townCenterSellInterval": 24`. The `kaggriculture.json` default is **12**, and `verify.py` calls `make("kaggriculture", configuration={"seed": seed})`, which takes the default. **Every benchmark, mirror, screen and gate this project has ever run used twice the competition's town-centre demand.**
- **The mechanism, exactly.** `_town_consume` fires the town centre on `step % center_interval == 0`: 30 ticks a season at 24 against 60 at 12, times the 1/2/4 decade multiplier. Town-centre drain per product is **70 units in the competition, 140 locally**. `townShopSellInterval` is 4 in both, so shop drain is unaffected — which is why the distortion is *per product*, in proportion to how much of its demand is the town centre rather than shops: **MELON 100%** (no shop lists it), WOOL and EGG 26%, MILK 19%, STRAWBERRY 15%, WHEAT 12%, FERTILIZER 0% (drained by nothing either way).
- **What it costs, measured on the unmodified v14 baseline, six-seed mirror:** interval 12 gives **127,114** and $153,387 of revenue a farm; interval 24 gives **99,419** and $125,157. Per product, units and $/unit: STRAWBERRY 256.8@$222 -> 211.8@$189, MILK 162.8@$232 -> 157.0@$207, **WOOL 142.5@$154 -> 160.0@$112**, MELON 71.2@$245 -> 71.4@$222.
- **Wool is the product it hides.** At interval 24 wool crosses `I0` on day 7, its `sq` curve takes the quote to **$5 on day 17**, and it sits below the $130 `SELL_RULES` floor from day 16 to day 25 — 20-41 units parked in the shed until the last bell. Locally it ends *below* `I0` at $236 and the floor is almost always satisfied. Every "wool is the hedge / wool is at its cliff" argument below was measured on the wrong side of that cliff.
- **How to apply.** `verify.py` and `loop.py` are frozen and must not be edited, so the gate cannot be moved. Instead: **run every candidate at both intervals, and disbelieve any gain concentrated in a town-centre-drained product.** A candidate that gains at 12 and not at 24 is an artefact of the harness, exactly the way a racing gain is an artefact of the mirror. This is a *third* blind spot, and unlike the mirror's two it belongs to the benchmark rather than to self-play.
- **Evidence that it bites immediately:** `MELON_LAST_DAY = 19` scored 12/16 head-to-head and **+$4,270 of mirror mean at interval 12** and **-$74 at interval 24** — see the melon entry below. Melon is drained by the town centre and nothing else, so it is precisely the product whose demand the mis-set constant doubles, and the entire local gain was fiction.
- **And it now bites in the other direction, 2026-08-16, on the largest number this project has measured.** Cutting the wheat field (`WHEAT_PER_ANIMAL` 0.75 -> 0.5 *with* `ENDGAME_WHEAT_TILES` 10 -> 0, which is not the old 0.5 test — `max(round(n*0.5), 10 if day>=20)` means 0.5 alone still leaves 10 tiles from day 20) is **+$9,735 a farm at interval 24 and -$1,068 at interval 12** over the same six seeds, and the four-seed gate scored it **4/8, +$108, mirror -$10,330**. So the harness refuses a change worth ~2.4 SE in the real market. **Do not assume the distortion only inflates local gains; it also hides real ones, and the wheat field is the standing example.**
- **The mechanism is *not* the wheat price.** Measured over six seeds, wheat is identical at both intervals (mean $40, tracking $25 -> $52 on the same path), while everything we sell is 10-12% cheaper at 24: strawberry $199 -> $181, milk $215 -> $196, wool $166 -> $143, melon $251 -> $225. Only the *sell* side moves, so a distortion argument has to run through what we produce, never through what we buy.
- **Confirmed on a disjoint sample, 2026-08-16, and the wheat cut is now the project's clearest case.** The queued screen was run: `WHEAT_PER_ANIMAL = 0.5` + `ENDGAME_WHEAT_TILES = 0` on **seeds 6-17, twelve seeds the gate has never touched**, is **122,945 -> 129,065 (+$6,119) at interval 12** and **107,132 -> 109,100 (+$1,968) at interval 24**. Pooled over all eighteen seeds it is **+$3,723 at 12 and +$4,557 at 24 — positive at both**. The -$1,068 on seeds 0-5 was the sample. The gate still refuses it (4/8, +$108, mirror -$10,330, reproduced to the dollar), and widening the head-to-head to eight seeds at interval 12 gives **9/16, +$113** — the same evidence v13 shipped on before gaining 23 rating points.
- **And both benchmark modes are biased against this particular change, which is new.** The cut replaces grown feed with bought feed, so it raises our `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT` volume. In the **mirror** both copies bid harder for the same scarce input and both pay the higher quote (the buying side of blind spot two). In the **head-to-head** the bid is unilateral — we pay the higher price and the baseline free-rides. Against the real field neither holds: **Jules alone buys 1,149 units of feed**. The one market this change moves is one the benchmark models as ours alone.
- **A second change now shows the same signature, 2026-08-16.** `RUSH_SHEEP = 2` (against the shipped 4) is **121,088 at interval 12 (-$6,026) and 105,116 at interval 24 (+$5,698)**, 12 mirror games a cell; `RUSH_SHEEP = 3` is -$5,963/+$2,510. Predicted before measuring, from combined wool supply being **320 units against a 268-unit drain at interval 24 — a glut that does not exist locally**. Head-to-head at interval 12 it is 2/16. So three independent changes now flip sign on this one configuration line (wheat field, opening sheep count, and the melon window in the other direction), and the two live ones are worth **+$9,735 and +$5,698 a farm at the competition's demand**.
- **And the config-gap changes do not stack, 2026-08-17, which closes this as a *submission* route.** The obvious move was to ship the interval-24 optimum as a package. Measured over **eighteen seeds, 36 games a cell**: `WHEAT_PER_ANIMAL = 0.5` + `ENDGAME_WHEAT_TILES = 0` + `RUSH_SHEEP = 2` together is **105,655 against 104,561 at interval 24 (+$1,094, ~0.6 SE) and 122,693 against 124,335 at interval 12 (-$1,642)** — against +$4,557 and +$5,698 for the halves measured separately. **Strongly sub-additive: the pair is worth less than either half.** Both cut the same thing from two ends — the sheep cut removes the wool the wheat field's feed was supporting, and the wheat cut replaces grown feed with bought feed for a herd the sheep cut has already shrunk. **The interval-24 optimum is a different agent, not a patch to this one.** Stop looking for a stack of constants that clears the gate; the individual measurements stand as facts about the real market and nothing more.
- **Required pairing:** nothing. Like reading the environment source, this is free and should precede every future hypothesis.
- **Reconsider if:** the competition's configuration changes. Re-read it from a fresh replay's `configuration` block rather than assuming; it is one line and it has already cost this project twenty-five experiments' worth of calibration.

### Keep livestock growth synchronized with capacity

- **Strategy:** Buy an animal only when one matching structure slot is ready, plan capacity at 9 animals per structure, and reserve 8 hands for feed transport.
- **Why it works:** It prevents the previous pattern of buying 33–46 animals while finishing with only 13–18, and avoids building 31–37 empty structures.
- **Evidence:** The repaired v5 agent beat the exact v4 baseline in 8/8 local games, averaging 78,578 versus 39,723 (+38,855). A representative game bought and kept 19 animals with no empty structures; the old agent bought 42, kept 6, and left 44 structures empty.
- **Required pairing:** Workload-based hiring, nearby placement, enough feed carriers, and no speculative construction.
- **Amended 2026-08-12:** the synchronisation is right for days 9-29 and wrong for days 0-8, where it costs more in production days than it saves in waste - see "Buy the herd in an opening rush" below. Capacity planning still governs after the window; the rush is bounded by cash and by `HERD_RUSH_SIZE` precisely so it cannot become the old overbuying.
- **Reconsider if:** A crop-heavy plan can prove that temporarily unused capacity returns more money than the synchronized pipeline.

### Minimize walking and protect the final day

- **Strategy:** Place new structures near the shed, hire for measured workload, stop FEED/CARE work on the final day, and reserve a cleanup crew for harvesting and selling.
- **Why it works:** Productive actions and end inventory matter more than theoretical capacity; row-major far-away construction consumed too many turns and left no hands available at the end.
- **Evidence:** This was part of the v5 package that improved public rating from 554.1 to 608.6 and passed the 8/8 local comparison above.
- **Required pairing:** The synchronized livestock pipeline; placement alone cannot fix overbuying.
- **Reconsider if:** A future path planner measures that travel is no longer the binding cost.

### Exploit wheat scarcity without displacing the herd

- **Strategy:** Maintain about 0.75 wheat tiles per animal, target 10 wheat tiles late, keep a feed reserve of `animal count + FEED_BUFFER`, and sell only true surplus (up to 12 units when price is at least 20).
- **Why it works:** Strong opponents both produce and trade wheat while livestock agents must buy it for feed. The 0.75 ratio captures crop value while preserving the reliable herd economy.
- **Evidence:** The 0.75 candidate won 8/8 against v5 and averaged 78,591 versus 70,656 (+7,934). Public v6 submission 55413328 then improved the rating from 608.8 to 660.2, validating the direction against the real field.
- **Required pairing:** Conservative field size, feed reserve, surplus-only selling, and the existing livestock labor model.
- **Amended 2026-08-16, and this is the run's headline: 0.75 is right for the benchmark and wrong for the competition.** `WHEAT_PER_ANIMAL = 0.5` **with `ENDGAME_WHEAT_TILES = 0`** — and note that is a different change from the old 0.5 test, because `_wheat_target` takes `max(round(n * 0.5), 10 if day >= 20)`, so 0.5 alone still leaves a 13-head herd 10 tiles from day 20 and never cut the late field — scores **+$9,735 a farm at interval 24 and -$1,068 at interval 12** over six seeds, and **4/8, +$108, mirror -$10,330** on the four-seed gate. 0.25 is +$6,876/-$746; **0.0 collapses at both** (~110,533/~91,086), so the feed a small field grows is real and only the *last* tiles are the waste.
- **The arithmetic behind it, and it is why this should be believed over the gate.** A wheat tile is ~5 actions for 4-6 units at $40-52, about **$36 an action against a farm average of $64**, and ten of them consume 20 of the 216 `slots` the crew can plan. Wheat's `below_func` is `sqrt` with amp 1.0, so 240 extra units of combined demand move the quote about **$4** — feed is nearly price-inelastic while the tile it frees is not. The field agrees from both ends: Jules ($154,073) plants **5** wheat tiles and buys **1,149** units of feed; Vaibhav Patel ($152,962) plants **130** and sells the surplus. Both beat us; neither runs our middle.
- **The 12-seed screen was run, 2026-08-16, and it says yes.** On seeds 6-17 the cut is **+$6,119 at interval 12** and **+$1,968 at interval 24**; pooled over seeds 0-17 it is **+$3,723 / +$4,557, positive at both**. The interval-12 loss was the seeds-0-5 sample. The field agrees from both flanks in the *new* replays too: **Jules ($154,073) plants 4-5 wheat tiles and buys 1,149 units of feed; Vaibhav Patel ($152,962) plants 93 and sells the surplus; we plant 40 and sell 117.** The action budget is where it shows: on the same crew and the same two land buys, Jules spends **PLANT 69 / WATER 672 / FERTILIZE 75** against our **PLANT 91 / WATER 540 / FERTILIZE 93**, and gets **6.35 units a berry tile against our 5.2**.
- **And the substitute repair loses, so the field is the right thing to cut and not the fertilizer.** Redirecting wheat's ~40 fertilizes to the berries (`fertilize = production_day`, dropping the wheat branch) is **-$8,653 at interval 12 and +$2,407 at 24**, and beside the cut it adds $1,400 at 24 for $2,850 at 12. Unfertilized wheat reaches 4 of a cap of 6, so the farm buys the difference back at $45: **v8's fertilize-the-wheat win was never about the fertilizer, it was about not bidding for feed** — which is exactly what the cut concedes on purpose. The two are substitutes, and cutting the field is the better half.
- **Reconsider if:** nothing further on the hypothesis; it is confirmed twice on disjoint samples. What is missing is a *gate* that can express it — one running at `townCenterSellInterval: 24`, or a head-to-head sample larger than four seeds.

### Water only when the expected crop return justifies the action

- **Strategy:** Water every other day before the crop bonus window, then water daily during bonus/production periods.
- **Why it works:** It saves low-value early actions while retaining mature crop output.
- **Evidence:** Watering alone was only marginal, but paired with the wheat loop it contributed to the 8/8, +7,934 v6 result.
- **Required pairing:** A profitable crop plan. Water optimization without crop economics is not a full strategy.
- **Reconsider if:** Crop timing or scoring rules change.

### Use a hard evidence gate before spending a submission

- **Strategy:** Compare candidate and exact baseline over at least four deterministic seeds in both seats; require 7/8 wins, at least +100 mean final money, all agents DONE, and passing tests. Before submission, the wrapper independently reruns a frozen verifier instead of trusting the agent's reported scores. A rejection must immediately feed a distinct next hypothesis rather than end the improvement loop.
- **Why it works:** It filters seat luck and attractive one-seed results while preserving the five-push budget, then converts failure evidence into the next experiment.
- **Evidence:** Several plausible crop variants were rejected locally; only the measured v6 improvement was submitted. The later two-quadrant cap won 7/8 but gained only +1,327.9, proving that the gate protected the submission budget while its routing diagnosis supplied the next search direction.
- **Required pairing:** Exact pre-run code and verifier snapshots, honest logging of rejected attempts, removal of rejected code, and a persistent `needsImprovement` state so the next three-hour run continues even without new replays.
- **Reconsider if:** The simulator becomes nondeterministic enough to require a larger sample.

### Never put a price floor on a product the town does not drain

- **Strategy:** Sell fertilizer as it is collected (`SELL_RULES["FERTILIZER"] = (3, 5)`) instead of holding it for a $50 quote that never returns.
- **Why it works:** A floor is a bet on price recovery. Fertilizer is the one good this farm gluts - 292 units a game against milk's 135 - and no shop drains it, so its price falls all season and ends near $1. The floor did not avoid selling into a crash, it guaranteed selling at the very bottom on day 29. It also freed about half the 100-item shed, which had been sitting under 45-53 fertilizer from day 20 and tipping the farm into `SHED_PRESSURE`, where the emergency rule dumps milk and wool at four times cap and ignores their floors.
- **Evidence:** Won 7/8 against the exact baseline, averaging $76,529.9 versus $75,026.0 (+$1,503.9), every status DONE; only loss was -$56. Measured revenue table for one game: MILK $226.1/unit ending at $238, WOOL $246.5 ending at $247, MELON $223.2 ending at $223, FERTILIZER $45.7 ending at $1.
- **Required pairing:** Keep collecting the fertilizer. The complementary attempt that gated *collection* on the same $50 floor lost 0/8 at -$8,435.6; the waste was the hoarding, never the production.
- **And the converse holds for a good the town *does* drain: do not remove the wool floor, 2026-08-16.** This ledger recorded, as an open leak, that at interval 24 wool quotes **$5 by day 17** and sits below its $130 floor from day 16 to day 25 with 20-41 units parked in the shed, feeding `SHED_PRESSURE`. Dropping the floor to 1 scores **+$434 at interval 12 (noise) and -$2,051 at interval 24**; to 60, -$1,513 / **-$5,171**. **The parked wool is not a leak — it is the correct response to a `sq` good in glut**: selling into the day-16-to-25 trough realises the bottom of its own curve, while holding to the day-20 4x town drain and the last bell realises more even after the pressure dumps. The rule is therefore not "never floor" but **floor exactly what something drains, and only that**: fertilizer has no drain and no recovery to wait for, wool has both.
- **Reconsider if:** A shop unlock starts draining fertilizer, or its price stops decaying across the season.

### Local self-play overvalues racing an opponent to a shared pool

- **Lesson:** `verify.py` plays the candidate against a copy of the baseline in the same market. Any change that simply acts *sooner* than the opponent on a shared, finite resource wins locally by beating a slower copy of itself, and that edge does not exist against a field that already acts promptly.
- **Evidence:** v7 dropped the fertilizer sell floor from $50 to $5 and won 7/8 locally at +$1,503.9. Its public rating settled at **649.0, below v6's 665.4**. Fertilizer is the clearest case in the game: nothing anywhere drains it, so its price is a strictly decreasing shared pool, and selling faster only moves who gets the top of a curve both farms are pushing down.
- **The detector, now in `verify.py` and gated by the wrapper:** `mirror` plays each agent against *itself* and reports its absolute score. Racing gains vanish in a mirror because both sides act at the same moment; production gains survive. The wrapper requires a mirror gain of at least 500.
- **How to apply:** Before trusting a local win, ask whether the gain comes from *producing or saving more* or merely from *getting there first*. More seeds do not fix this - it is a bias in the benchmark's design, not its sample size.
- **Reconsider if:** A racing change ever clears the mirror gate; then raise the threshold.

### The mirror has a second blind spot: mutual restraint

- **Lesson:** the mirror was built to catch *racing* - a change that only beats a slower copy of itself - and it does. But it plays **two copies of the candidate**, so a change that **reduces supply** reduces it on both sides of a shared market, prices rise for both farms, and the change credits itself with revenue that exists only because the opponent restrained too. Against a field that keeps flooding the market, those prices never appear.
- **Evidence (2026-08-13):** the land utilisation gate (`empty_owned < 8` before buying a quadrant) scored **8/16 wins, mean +$70.2, mirror +$3,118.4** - a coin flip against a real opponent while clearing the mirror threshold six times over. Its mirror mean, $122,640, is the highest number this project has ever measured, and it is an artefact.
- **How to apply - the direction of the disagreement names the artefact:**
  - head-to-head **up**, mirror **flat** -> racing. v7: +$1,504 and +$84, shipped, **-36 rating**.
  - head-to-head **flat**, mirror **up** -> mutual restraint. This entry.
  - both up -> evidence. v9 +$10,041/+$11,235, v10 +$9,648/+$9,671, v11 +$18,552/+$18,468, v12 +$17,391/+$5,533 - every shipped winner agrees on both.
- **Required pairing:** read them as a pair, always. A candidate that clears one gate and not the other has told you *which* artefact it is, which is worth more than the number.
- **Amended 2026-08-13 - restraint has a buying side too.** `per = 1` in `_fetch_jobs` (one wheat per carrier instead of two) scored **2/8, -$614.9 head-to-head, mirror +$1,656.9**. It sells nothing differently; it changes how much wheat the farm pulls out of the shed, and `_market` sizes `BUY_PRODUCT` off `carried`, so it changes how hard the farm bids for feed. Wheat is the one input both farms buy, sits in scarcity all season (measured depth 897, price $55 against a $25 base) and rises on a `sqrt` curve. Two copies that both bid less both get the calmer price; against a baseline that bids the old way we pay its price and keep only the disadvantage. **So the rule is not "restraint in selling" - it is any change that moves a shared price, in either direction, for both copies at once.** Before believing a mirror gain, ask which market price the change moves and check that quantity on both sides.
- **Amended 2026-08-15 - the sharpest case of blind spot two yet, and it names the general rule.** Late price floors keyed on the shop lottery (`SELL_LATE_FLOORS = {WOOL: 200, MILK: 200}` from day 12) scored **mirror +$2,449 at six seeds, positive on five of six - and 4/8 head-to-head at -$2,945**. Seed 3 alone is +$2,000 in the mirror and **-$12,600 in both seats** head-to-head. Nothing is withheld in total: everything is dumped at the last bell either way. It is *purely* a timing change, and it still fails, because in the mirror both farms hold, the town drain restores the price and both collect it, while against a baseline that keeps selling the opponent's supply sets the price anyway and we sell into their glut late with no early cash to show for it. **You cannot wait out a glut in a market you only half supply.** Generalise it: a change is safe from this blind spot only if it moves what we *produce*, not when we release it.
- **A third caution, on the benchmark rather than the mirror, 2026-08-15.** The six-seed baseline mirror measured **127,114.2, 127,304.7 and 126,839.3** in three separate processes, with individual games differing by up to **$2,315**. `env.run` is not bit-reproducible across processes here, so re-measure the baseline in the same process as the candidate. **Superseded on the size of the noise floor by the entry below - it is not $1,000, it is $3,200, and it comes from chaos rather than from the process.**
- **Reconsider if:** never. Both blind spots follow from the mirror being two copies of one agent; neither is fixable by more seeds.

### The benchmark is deterministic and chaotic, and the mirror gate is 0.11 of a standard error

- **Lesson:** a change with **zero economic content** moves this benchmark by more than most real changes do. Measured 2026-08-15 by flipping `_step_toward` to step along y before x instead of x before y - same Manhattan distance, same priorities, same jobs, same market, a pure tie-break and its own mirror image. Six-seed mirror **-$8,498.1** on seeds 0-5 and **-$3,339.9** on seeds 6-11. A second neutral rule (step along the longer axis first) scored **+$131.9** and **-$6,367.8** on the same two sets.
- **The numbers to carry forward.** Per-seed delta SD from those two runs: **$7,905 and $9,721**. The mirror runs one game per seed, so **SE of a six-seed mirror delta is ~$3,200-$4,000**, and of the four-seed mirror `verify.py` reports, **~$4,000-$4,900**. The +$500 mirror threshold is **~0.11 SE**. Resolving a true +$1,000 at 95% confidence needs on the order of **240 seeds**.
- **This is chaos, not measurement noise, and the distinction decides what to do about it.** A null candidate - `main.py` byte-identical to the baseline - through `verify.py` returns **2 wins, 2 losses, 4 exact ties, mean delta $0, mirror delta exactly 0.0**, every game bit-reproducible. Re-running does not help. Only more *seeds* help.
- **And the four seeds the gate actually uses are not a fair sample of the baseline, 2026-08-16.** `verify.py 4` runs seeds 0-3. The baseline's interval-12 mirror on exactly those four is **131,910 against its own six-seed mean of 127,114** — seeds 0 and 3 are its two luckiest games at that config (148,323 and 137,574 against a ~125,000 median for seeds 1, 2 and 5). A null candidate still ties exactly, so this is not bias in the *delta*; it is that any candidate which perturbs the market at all hands back $20k of baseline luck on half the gate's sample. Measured on the wheat cut at seed 0: it loses **$21,876** while **strawberry 277 -> 268.5 units, cow 165 -> 166.5, sheep 144 -> 137.5, herd 13 -> 13, berries 40 -> 40 and the crew is unchanged** — no output quantity moved at all. **Before believing a large negative on the gate, check whether it is concentrated on seeds 0 and 3, and re-screen on seeds outside 0-5.**
- **How to apply, three rules.**
  1. **The 7/8 head-to-head ratio is the only strong filter in the gate.** It is paired - both farms in one game on one market path - its null is 4/8 with four exact ties, and 7-of-8 under noise alone is ~3.5%. Read the mirror for *sign on a large effect* and for its two blind spots; do not read it as a threshold.
  2. **Disbelieve any screen reported at $1,000-$3,000 of six-seed mirror mean, in either direction.** That covers `HERD_MAX = {"SHEEP": 4}` (+$836), `ANIMAL_SLOTS = 6` with caps (+$1,426), the day-12 shop mix (+$298), `CASH_FLOOR = 50` (-$428), `FEED_DAYS_EARLY = 1` (-$1,543), `BERRY_TILES = 48` (-$992) and much of the 2026-08-13 twenty-screen sweep. Screens past about **$6,000** carry information; the ones below never did.
  3. **A conditional policy that fires in a minority of games cannot pass this gate**, because a tie is not a win. That is a property of the gate, not of the policy, and it is what refuses both the shop-conditioned herd mix and `HERD_MAX`.
- **It also explains the shape of this project's history:** v13 shipped on 9/16 with a mirror of +$1,407 (0.3 SE, i.e. nothing) and its rating rose 23 points anyway, while several 7/8 winners with mirrors above $8,000 are the changes that actually moved the agent. The ratio was carrying the evidence the whole time.
- **Required pairing:** screen on the paired head-to-head win count at eight seeds when the expected effect is small; keep the cheap six-seed mirror only for the blind-spot check and for effects above $6,000.
- **Reconsider if:** never. This is a property of a greedy dispatcher on a shared market, and more seeds is the only lever.

### Submitting retires an older agent, so do not submit faster than ratings settle

- **Lesson:** Only the two most recent submissions keep playing episodes; Kaggle retires the rest, and the competition page shows only the live ones. A retired agent's rating freezes wherever it happened to be. The binding constraint on submitting is therefore **not** the five-a-day budget - it is that a new submission replaces an agent that may not have finished converging, so you never learn whether it was good.
- **Evidence (2026-08-12 00:45 UTC):** eight submissions exist in the API; only v8 (31 episodes, last 00:15) and v7 (30, last 23:15) were still playing. v6's final episode was 17:33, two minutes before v8 was submitted at 17:35. Everything from v5 back had stopped hours to days earlier.
- **Not established:** the exact retirement rule. v5 kept playing long after v6 landed, so it is not a strict "newest two". Do not assume a rule; check `kaggle competitions episodes <id>` for the most recent episode timestamp.
- **How to apply:** Before spending a submission, check that the current agent has stopped moving (roughly 30+ episodes and a stable score). Shipping a marginal candidate costs the evidence for the one already in flight.

### Never read a public rating before it has converged

- **Lesson:** A fresh submission's rating swings wildly for its first dozen episodes and means nothing. Wait for roughly 25-30 episodes (`kaggle competitions episodes <id>`) before treating a rating as evidence.
- **Evidence:** v8 has read **777.0 at 7 episodes**, **653.7 at 28**, and **682.8 at 31**. A conclusion was drawn and written into this ledger off the 7-episode number, then corrected off the 28-episode number, and both were premature: the score swung 124 points and was still moving at 31 games.
- **Standings at 2026-08-12 00:45 UTC:** v8 682.8 (31 games, live), v6 665.4 (42, retired), v7 651.6 (30, live), v5 602.7 (38, retired). v8 is probably the best agent shipped so far, but "probably" is the honest word until it stops moving.
- **The uncomfortable implication:** the local benchmark reported +$1,504 for v7 and +$3,567 for v8; the mirror reported +84 and +3,903. The mirror's *ordering* has held up - v7 flat-to-down, v8 up - but the magnitudes do not map onto rating at all, and the field still contains farms scoring twice our best. Chasing the field ceiling remains worth more than another local delta.
- **Resolved 2026-08-14, and it went the other way.** v13 was shipped with the ratio gate knowingly unmet (9/16, mean +$150, mirror +$1,407) on the argument that a ~1.2% effect cannot be resolved by 8 games. Its rating settled at **859.5, up from v12's 813.9**. So a 9/16 that both gates agree on is not automatically noise, and the wrapper's 7/8 ratio is a *submission* gate, not a truth: it is the right rule to spend a submission on, but a candidate it refuses is not thereby disproven. This is the standing reason the `HERD_RUSH_DAY = 10` + `HERD_RUSH_SIZE = 10` pair (5/8, +$2,933, mirror +$3,252) is still queued rather than closed.
- **How to apply:** Record the episode count beside every rating in these ledgers, and say "still moving" rather than picking a number. A rating without its episode count is not evidence. See also the entry above on retirement: a rating stops converging the moment its agent is retired.

### Read the environment source, not the agent's own comments

### Match the closest (unit, job) pair, not the nearest unit to each job

### Measure the farm before theorising about it

- **Strategy:** Instrument a real game - action histogram, direction reversals, PASS causes, per-product revenue - before choosing a hypothesis.
- **Why it works:** Three consecutive plausible efficiency changes failed because the diagnosis was inherited rather than measured. One 30-second instrumented game replaced "movement is the bottleneck" with the actual numbers and pointed straight at the revenue side, where no experiment had ever looked.
- **Evidence:** The queued route-ownership hypothesis assumed re-targeting waste; measurement found 185 reversals in 3,462 moves (5.3%). PASS analysis found 71% of idle units were empty-handed with open jobs available, so idleness was a carrying constraint, not a work shortage. The revenue table then produced the +$1,503.9 winner on the first try.
- **Required pairing:** Throwaway instrumentation outside the agent (wrap `_assign` or `_market`), never edits to `main.py` or `verify.py` for measurement.
- **First, the replay's index is off by one, 2026-08-16, and getting this wrong under-reads a season by 96%.** `steps[i]["action"]` is the action that **produced** `steps[i]["observation"]`, not one applied to it — the interpreter mutates the state in place and the framework records action and result together. Pair `steps[i-1]`'s observation with `steps[i]`'s action. Reconstructed the wrong way, Jules's $154,073 season reads as $6,331 of net trading; the right way it reads $173,928 against an exact residual of $174,218.
- **And two counting traps that point in opposite directions.** Counting `private["seeds"]` *increases* under-counts, because a seed bought and planted in the same turn nets out (Jules reads 32 strawberry that way and bought 46); counting `BUY_SEED` order quantities is exact and cross-checks against `PLANT` action counts (Jules 46+18+5 = 69 seeds, 69 PLANT; ours 40+40+12 = 92, 91 PLANT). Counting shed *increases* for animals over-counts, because a carrier that cannot place a beast drops it back in.
- **And the agent is nowhere near the step limit, so stop wondering about the low-score tail.** `kaggriculture.json` sets **`actTimeout: 1`**, and eleven of 64 v14 games score under $62,000 with the *opponent* also low — the signature of a truncated episode. Measured with `perf_counter` around `agent()` over a full episode: **mean 0.2 ms, p99 1.2 ms, max 6.0 ms, nothing above 0.5 s.** Three orders of magnitude of headroom. The tail is variance, not our runtime.
- **How to read an opponent's replay, 2026-08-16, because the obvious way is wrong by 40%.** Counting `SELL` order quantities over-reads revenue badly: a multi-unit SELL is capped by what is actually in the shed, so a policy that orders `["SELL", "FERTILIZER", 60]` every turn looks like 2,245 units sold. - **Attempt:** an instrumented v13 mirror logs what blocks the next animal on all 720 turns, and days 16-20 are `slots` on **24 turns of 24**. - **The one axis left, and it is the standing next hypothesis, 2026-08-17.** All four refusals cut the *carrier count* or the *demand*, and the result was monotone in the size of the buffer — which is why the buffer is the mechanism and not a rationalisation. **Nobody has held the buffer fixed and removed only the top-up churn.** `_fetch_jobs` sizes demand as `n_animals - sum(carried)`, so the instant any single unit spends its wheat on a FEED it reopens a one-unit shortfall and the crew tops itself back up: ~13 redundant pickups a day, 479 pickups hauling ~607 units to deliver 283 feeds, and 324 units riding back into the shed at nightfall. Size the demand off the **number of units holding at least one wheat** instead of off total units carried, which preserves parallel delivery capacity exactly while refusing to re-fetch for a carrier that is already loaded. A quadrant bought a day sooner is bought out of the same cash desert the opening rush is spending, and the herd is the better claim on it. **Land is now refused from both ends — earlier, later, more and fewer — and none of the five experiments moved the score.**

### Multi-dock pickups without persistent route ownership

### Static reduction of the cash feed reserve

- **Attempt:** Reduce `FEED_DAYS` from 10 to 8 because public replays retained 96.6% of purchased animals and the wheat loop supplies part of feed demand.
- **Evidence:** Won 3/8 and averaged 72,854.6 versus 71,839.0 (+1,015.6), with every status DONE; two pairings gained more than $6,600 while four lost, so the effect was strongly condition-dependent and far below the gate overall.
- **Why it failed in this pairing:** A fixed reserve cut releases useful growth capital when wheat supply is secure, but removes necessary insurance when timing, prices, or carrier throughput are worse. High aggregate survival does not identify which seed-level reserves are redundant.
- **Resolved 2026-08-12, and the answer was the calendar, not the inventory.** Both this and the "secured wheat" attempt below were season-long rules, and season-long is the wrong axis: cutting `FEED_DAYS` to 3 or 5 for the whole game swings a single seed by +$5k and -$17k, i.e. noise. The reserve only ever binds in the opening, where it is the sole thing freezing the herd - see the worked entry "Buy the herd in an opening rush" above, which cuts it to three days for days 0-8 only and is worth +$18,552. Do not re-test a season-long reserve rule.
- **Confirmed properly 2026-08-17, on the one channel that made it look worth re-opening.** The 2026-08-12 refusal was a single-seed screen, and the reserve has a second job nobody had traced: **`BUY_SEED STRAWBERRY` spends `budget`, and `budget = money - CASH_FLOOR - n_animals * keep`** with `keep` the live wheat quote times `FEED_DAYS`. At day 12 with 10 head and wheat at $35 that is **$3,500 held back — thirty-five berry seeds — on exactly the days the berry field stalls at 34 of 40 tiles**, and the reserve is never actually spent from `budget` because feed is bought out of `upkeep`. Screened at six seeds and both intervals anyway: `FEED_DAYS = 6` is **-$1,928 / -$5,460** and `FEED_DAYS = 3` is **-$103 / -$3,329**. Negative at both settings at both intervals. **The diagnosis was right about the channel and wrong about the remedy** — the reserve is what stops a herd bought on credit walking off, and releasing it season-long puts capital into a herd the farm cannot feed through the day-15 wheat squeeze. The refusal now rests on twelve games a cell instead of one.

### Treating all secured wheat as cash-reserve replacement

- **Attempt:** Keep the ten-day horizon but subtract every wheat unit already in the shed or on a carrier from the current herd's cash reserve; retain the full ten-day marginal reserve for new animals.
- **Evidence:** Won 5/8 and averaged 72,441.0 versus 71,400.1 (+1,040.9), with all statuses DONE. The public wheat-loop rating still rose from 602.7 to 659.6, and six new replays ended with 7-18 wheat, so the underlying wheat strategy remains validated even though this capital rule missed the gate.

The change accelerated growth in favorable pairings but lost three games and stayed far below +3,000.
- **Test again only with:** Day-indexed feed coverage that deducts only wheat beyond current-day delivery needs, or harvest timing proven to cover the reserve horizon. Conditional reserve release is not universally bad; raw aggregate inventory is too coarse.

### Fixed cow-heavy herd under shared dynamic prices

- **Attempt:** Shift the herd target from 10% goose / 50% cow / 40% sheep to 10% / 70% / 20% because public replays showed higher final milk prices and more milk sold than wool.
- **Evidence:** Won 2/8 and averaged 71,856.9 versus 74,442.8 (-2,585.9), with every status DONE.
- **Why it failed in this pairing:** The observed milk advantage was endogenous to diversified supply; extra cows pushed the linear milk glut curve down, while removing sheep surrendered the wool hedge.
- **Confirmed again on v13, 2026-08-13, and the ceiling is the price curve.** Value per animal-day, measured by patching `_daily_refresh_animals` (278 animal-days, 5 uncared, 11 clipped - execution is near-perfect): **COW $259, SHEEP $228, GOOSE $104.** Geese really are the worst tile on the farm, and removing them still loses: 0/55/45 is **-$1,430** of mirror mean, 0/70/30 is **-$17,350**. Milk's `below_func` is `sqrt` with amp 8.69 and it ends only 109 units below `I0`, so three more cows crash the very price that made them look good, and the geese that go were also the wool hedge. **10/50/40 is not a guess, it is the shape of three price curves.**
- **Do not confuse this with the opening order, 2026-08-14.** This entry is about the *target mix*, which is fixed by three price curves and is correct. Which species is bought **first** is a separate question with the opposite answer: `RUSH_SHEEP = 4` is worth +$6,989/+$8,687 - see "Buy the herd in an opening rush" above. A mix change moves supply and the mirror lies about it; an order change does not and the mirror does not.
- **Closed from four directions 2026-08-14, and wool is the reason.** Five hypotheses were screened on the v14 baseline and *four of them moved the score through one number*, wool: baseline **288 units at $210 each**; +52 units -> **$80**; +111 units -> **$96**. Wool is the only product on `sq` (amp 0.058), so its marginal unit near our own volume is worth less than nothing once shed pressure starts dumping it below its floor. Milk is `sqrt` and still climbing at $286 on day 29. That reads like "buy cows, not sheep", and every version of that loses: `HERD = 10/60/30` (the one unscreened step, between two already-losing endpoints) is **0/4, -$4,694.2, mirror -$10,283.0**. Wool is simultaneously at its cliff *and* the hedge; the marginal cow crashes milk's `sqrt` from the other side. A cow being individually better ($259 an animal-day against $228) does not make a cow-heavier herd better.
- **Two mechanisms found while testing that, and both are load-bearing exactly as they stand.** (1) `_market` buys `want` **cheapest-first**, and COW is $400 against SHEEP's $500, so day 0 asks for `{SHEEP: 4, COW: 2}` and actually buys **2 cows and 3 sheep**. `RUSH_SHEEP = 4` is tuned *through* that sort: the quantity ever measured was never "four sheep", it was "three sheep and two cows". Replacing the sort with the scan's own order - which produces Suda's literal 4-sheep-1-cow opening - is **0/4, -$15,246.2, mirror -$34,773.8**, the largest single-change loss in this project, with wool at 399 units/$96 and milk collapsing 326 -> 155 units. (2) `ANIMALS["SHEEP"]["last"] = 8`, to stop buying sheep after the rush, is **5/8, +$145.5, mirror +$565.9** and is *almost a no-op*: on three of four seeds the candidate's score is identical to the baseline's, because the late sheep are bought inside the rush window and sit in the shed, where `_next_animal` returns held stock before it reaches the `last` test. A `last` day cannot cap an animal that was already paid for; only `RUSH_SHEEP` can.
- **Tested 2026-08-15 and pruned, and the refusal is the gate's rather than the market's.** `HERD` was made a function of `obs["town"]["unlocked_shops"]` (`_herd_mix` rebuilding a normalised mix each turn from the drain each species' product has, a single-product shop counting double). Rewarding a drained species is **-$12,619** on seed 0: wool is the one `sq` good and 144 units is already the whole drain, so tilting *toward* the winner overshoots its own cliff. One-sided instead - penalise a species no unlocked shop lists - is **-$6,199/-$3,809** at six seeds, and the per-seed pattern is exactly as predicted: seed 4, where YARN_STORE opens on **day 24**, gains **+$11,677** in a seat, while seeds 0-3, where it opens on days 12-18, lose. So the penalty is wrong before the draw is legible - a shop that has not opened is *early*, not absent, since eight of them unlock across days 3-24. Gating it to day 12+ fixes that and makes it **bit-identical to baseline on five of six seeds** (+$298 overall, all of it seed 4), because **the herd is already bought by then**: 10 head at day 12, 13 at day 17. The signal is real and correctly signed; there is simply almost no herd decision left open once the lottery can be read, and a candidate that ties in five games of six cannot reach 7/8. See the calibration entry above - this is the same wall `HERD_MAX` hit.
- **Every one of the five refusals above was measured on the wrong market, and this is the run's best distinct next hypothesis (2026-08-15).** At `townCenterSellInterval: 24` a cow is 1.5 units/day at $207 = **$310/day** and a sheep 1.33 at $112 = **$149/day**, a ratio of **2.1**; at 12 the same numbers are $348 and $205, a ratio of 1.7. Combined wool supply is **320 units against a 268-unit drain — a genuine glut that does not exist locally** — while combined milk is 309 against 367 and still in scarcity. So the two arguments this entry rests on ("wool is simultaneously at its cliff and the hedge", "the marginal cow crashes milk's `sqrt` from the other side") are both weaker in the real game than in the benchmark that refused them. **Hold `RUSH_SHEEP = 4` — the sheep-first opening is worth +$6,989 because sheep produce two days sooner, which is a calendar argument that survives the config change — and screen `HERD` toward cows at both intervals, requiring a gain at 24 and no loss at 12.** If it gains at 24 and loses at 12, that is the config gap again and the gate cannot ship the fix; record that and stop, rather than tuning until the wrong market approves.

The prompting evidence was as strong as it gets — the two $150k opponents in the new v14 replays (Jules 154,073, Vaibhav Patel 152,962) both run **zero geese** and 14-15 pasture animals — and it still loses, because what a leader's herd *is* does not tell you what our marginal head *displaces*. **Six mix refusals now. The mix is closed; stop re-deriving it from opponents' final herds.**
- **Seventh refusal, 2026-08-17, and it is the first that ever raised the goose share.** All six refusals above moved goose to **zero** or left it at 0.10; nobody had tried the other direction, and the interval-24 depth table says a goose is worth **$128 an animal-day against a sheep's $76**, costs $200 less and produces from day 4 rather than day 6 — three arguments pointing the same way in the cash desert. - **Test again only with:** a species decision still open on day 15+, or a gate that can score a policy on the games where it fires. Not with a new price argument, and not with a new opponent census. Superseded reasoning follows, kept for the evidence in it.
- **The earlier framing, 2026-08-15.** The re-test condition on record was "a shop rotation that changes which of milk/wool/egg the town drains". **That rotation happens every game and it is observable.** A shop unlocks every third day in a random order, and it is the only real drain in town; measured across six baseline mirrors, YARN_STORE's unlock day alone moves wool between **$207 and $84 a unit on an identical 140-unit supply** — a $17,741 swing (see "Read the environment source"). Screen it as: weight each species by the number of unlocked shops draining its product (a single-product shop such as YARN_STORE counting double, since it drains at twice the rate), floored so no species is driven to zero — the fixed 10/50/40 is itself a hedge against this lottery, and the hedge is what a live signal should replace, not abandon.
- **Superseded queued hypothesis:** the dock-zoning idea here is dead - with four quadrants unlocked the four shed docks are the centre 2x2, mutually adjacent, so dock choice is worth at most one move. That is why both dock experiments failed and why no third is warranted.

### Persistent per-unit job claims

- **Attempt:** Keep each unit walking to the tile it claimed until it arrives or the job disappears, instead of rebuilding every assignment from scratch each turn. This is the root-cause form of the queued route-ownership hypothesis, applied to all jobs rather than only carriers.
- **Evidence:** Won 1/8 and averaged $73,671.3 versus $75,956.8 (-$2,285.5), every status DONE. Instrumentation then showed only 185 opposite-direction moves out of 3,462 (5.3%), so there was almost no thrash to recover.
- **Why it failed in this pairing:** The premise was wrong, not the implementation. Recomputing assignments every turn is adaptivity, not waste: a claimed unit is withheld from a nearer or more urgent job that appeared after it set off.
- **Test again only with:** A measured reversal rate above roughly 20%. Persistence is not universally bad; there is simply nothing here for it to fix.

### Removing logistics waste without a job to replace it

- **Attempt:** Batch shed pickups into whole loads (`MIN_LOAD = 3`) after measuring 499 PICKUP actions against 308 actual deliveries, caused by demand being recomputed against instantaneous carried stock so every consumed wheat reopens a one-item shortfall.

This was read for four experiments as **labour supply is not the binding constraint on this farm**. - **Test again only with:** nothing foreseeable.

### Reading single-seed differences between variants as signal

- **Attempt:** Diagnosing seed 1, the one seed where fertilizing loses (both seats, about -$5,000), by comparing variants seed by seed.
- **Evidence:** Day-by-day tracing showed a real mechanism - fertilizer is still worth $89 on day 10, so early diversion costs cash that compounds into two fewer animals by day 15 and never recovers. - **Test again only with:** Eight seeds or more before believing a per-seed story. The 4-seed benchmark is a screen, not a verdict, for changes that move market prices.
